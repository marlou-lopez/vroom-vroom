# Track edges and kerbs from a shared-memory survey (#97)

**Question:** the detailed 2D view and the 3D ribbon rebuild track edges and kerbs from the two compared laps (#36). LMU reports the edge only on the side the car is on, so one side is measured on about half of the lap, and kerbs come from a 5 Hz any-wheel surface flag. Can a few laps recorded through shared memory (#94) give both edges and the kerbs for the whole track?

**Status:** experimental. In the app, the **Edges** toggle of the detailed view reads **Survey** instead of **Telemetry** when the compare response has a survey (Spa mock only), and the ribbon uses the same scene. **Decision: pending the user's keep / change / drop.**

**Second pass (wheel contacts):** LMU's `mTrackEdge` turned out to be the AI path's width stored per waypoint every 4 to 10 m (Bus Stop left side, lap distance to half-width: 6592 m 5.45, 6599 m 6.12, 6607 m 7.15, 6610 m 8.62, 6622 m 8.77, 6632 m 6.97, 6646 m 6.38, 6659 m 5.93; La Source's left side drops from about 7.3 to 3.0 m around 250 m), identical for every car, so the edge drawn from it looks squiggly. The edge is now the **physical edge from the wheels**: where a wheel's terrain name changes between racing tarmac and anything else, that wheel is on the edge. LMU's width is only a smoothed fallback where no wheel crossed.

## Method: how to survey a track

1. Practice session, logger running (`tools/lmu-logger/`; player-only is enough, `--all-cars` also adds the other cars' crossings and widths).
2. **For wheel contacts** (crossings are what count: a wheel running continuously off the tarmac gives a point only where it goes off and where it comes back; the second survey's two laps with the outer wheels over the edge gave about one crossing every 15 m per side, roughly 460 and 410 per lap; their contact laps took about 450 to 550 s each): drive one lap with the **left** wheels just over the edge (on the kerb, run-off or grass beside the white line) and the right wheels on the tarmac, at about 60 km/h, then the same lap with the **right** wheels over the edge. Skip gravel traps and walls. Every crossing in and out gives one point on the edge, so the more times the wheels go over the line, the better; a lap that weaves over the line is as good as one that stays beside it.
3. `python decode.py survey a.lmucap b.lmucap --out <track>-survey.json` (one streaming pass per file). Output: `edges` (LMU's widths), `kerbs`, `bank` (road banking for the cockpit camera, see `cockpit-view.md`), `contacts` (`edge` and `kerb_outer` points) and the `wheel_offsets` used and fitted. Details in the logger's README.
4. The mock serves `public/mock/spa-survey-2026-11-06.json` (from an 18-car all-cars capture and a player-only capture whose four laps ran about 1 m inside the edges). `src/lib/trackSurvey.ts` (`mapSurvey`) keeps the contacts near a lap as [lat, lon] points (unsided) and maps LMU's widths and the kerb hits to the 1 m grid (lat/lon to world x/z as in `elevation.ts`, nearest point of the lap polyline, dropped beyond 15 m); the response is `CompareResponse.track_survey`. It also carries `bank_deg` (#100): the road's banking in degrees per grid metre (positive = right side lower), the median of the nearest bank samples with gaps of up to 8 m interpolated, null elsewhere; on Spa 99.8% to 100% of the metres are measured. The bank samples come from every car on tarmac in the same captures, so a survey needs no extra driving.
5. The scene (`detailed/scene.ts`, helpers in `trackSurvey.ts`) turns that into edges and kerbs in the view's own frame. It projects every contact onto the scene's centreline (nearest segment within 20 m; the sign of the offset is the side, its size the half-width, so the drawn edge passes through the contacts even where the rebuilt centreline is off), drops outlier contacts (more than 0.75 m from the median of the side's contacts within 10 m; 5 left and 3 right on Spa), takes the median of the contacts within 1 m of each index, smooths them over 2 m (measured neighbours only), joins measured indices at most 30 m apart by a straight line between their half-widths ("bridged": LMU is ignored there), and fills the rest from LMU's width averaged over 15 m and shifted by an offset interpolated between the corrections at the two ends of the gap (each the median of contact minus smoothed LMU over the nearest 10 m of contacts). A side without contacts shows smoothed LMU as it is. A kerb run keeps its place from the kerb hits, sits with its inner side on the new edge, and is as wide as the median distance of the `kerb_outer` points beyond the edge (believed between 0.3 and 3 m, from at least three points), else 0.9 m.

## How wheel positions are found

- **Orientation convention** (checked on the player's capture, 52,000 samples above 20 m/s): `mOri` holds the rows of the vehicle-to-world matrix, `world_r = mOri[r] . local`, with vehicle +x out of the left side, +y up and +z out of the back, as LMU's header says. `mOri x (0, 0, -1)` has a dot product of 1.0000 with the direction of travel from position deltas (10th to 90th percentile all 1.0000); reading the matrix transposed gives a median of 0.43. `mOri x (1, 0, 0)` has a dot product of 0.9987 (median) with forward x up, i.e. it points to the left of travel in the left-handed y-up world. As a second check on left and right, contacts of the left wheels land on the side where LMU's own left width applies: on the grid the median distance to the same side's width is 0.17 m (left) and 0.29 m (right), against 0.90 and 1.05 m to the other side's.
- **Wheel positions are not in shared memory**: a wheel is the car's `mPos` plus the orientation times an offset (half-track 0.85 m assumed, front and rear axle z fitted). The half-track cannot be fitted from the data; an error of 5 cm moves every contact 5 cm across the edge.
- **Axle fit** (`contacts.py`): a rear wheel crosses the same edge as the front wheel of its side, so for the right offsets the two crossings coincide. 1,500 front/rear pairs (the front wheel's crossing followed within 1.2 s by the rear wheel's, same side, same direction, nothing else in between) were found in the two Spa captures. Many pairs do not coincide (a shallow crossing smears the rear one along the edge, kerbs have steps), so the fit counts pairs closer than 0.3 m instead of minimising a median. **Only the wheelbase is determined**: it is 2.80 m (prior 2.70 m; the score is flat from 2.6 to 2.8 m), with 389 pairs within 0.3 m (381 at the prior). Moving both axles together moves both crossings alike, so the split relative to `mPos` cannot be fitted this way; the axles keep the prior's midpoint (-1.40 m and +1.40 m). A wrong split shifts all contacts along the car's heading, not across the edge, so the edge's position across the road barely changes. Residual over the pairs closer than 1 m (767 of 1,500 at the fit, 798 at the prior): median 0.30 m, 90th percentile 0.86 m at the fit (0.33 and 0.86 at the prior).

## Verified numbers (Spa)

Wheel contacts (the 18-car capture plus the player's four edge laps, mapped on the 3 Nov recording's lap 4 against 3):

- **Contacts:** 11,359 wheel crossings, 6,438 points after the 0.25 m dedupe, 4,641 on the edge and 1,782 on kerbs' outer boundary near the lap (15 more are further than 15 m from the lap). The file is 532 KB.
- **Coverage:** wheels crossed the edge on **18.9% (left) and 20.1% (right)** of the 6,980 grid metres. The 18 cars drove the racing line and the player's four laps stayed about 1 m inside the white lines, so few wheels went over the edge; the rest of the lap is LMU's width. That is what the new capture (wheels just over the edge, one side per lap) is for.
- **How wrong LMU's width is:** where wheels measured the edge, |contact edge - LMU's width averaged over 15 m| has a median of 0.17 m (left) and 0.29 m (right), 90th percentile 0.81 and 0.84 m; against the raw LMU width 0.16 / 0.79 m (left) and 0.27 / 0.86 m (right). LMU's width is wider on average: contact minus LMU is -0.15 m (left) and -0.29 m (right) at the median. Its step between neighbouring metres is up to 5.04 m (left) and 4.51 m (right); the contact-based edge (first pass, before the second survey) stepped at most 0.73 m (left; at La Source) and 0.26 m (right).
- **The drawn edge passes through the contacts:** distance from each contact to the drawn edge at its index has a median of 0.02 m and a 90th percentile of 0.08 m (the remainder is the 2 m smoothing).
- **Second survey (three captures: all cars, survey laps, contact laps):** 5,272 edge points near the lap (2,506 left, 2,766 right), falling on 1,120 and 1,203 distinct grid metres. Wheels measured the edge on **21.9% (left) and 23.0% (right)** of the grid; |contact - LMU| median 0.20 / 0.24 m, 90th percentile 0.81 / 0.83 m. Crossings cluster: about half the lap has no crossing within 120 m on either side (gaps over 120 m total 3,505 m left, 3,596 m right).
- **Outliers:** front and rear crossings agree only to a median 0.32 m, 90th percentile 0.83 m, so a contact more than `CONTACT_OUTLIER_M` = 0.75 m from the median of its side's contacts within +/- 10 m (needs 3 in the window) is dropped before smoothing and bridging: **5 left, 3 right**.
- **Bridging (`CONTACT_BRIDGE_M` = 30 m):** between two measured indices at most 30 m apart the edge is a straight line, LMU ignored. Share of the grid in gaps up to 10 / 20 / 30 / 40 / 60 / 80 m: 5.8 / 9.5 / 11.8 / 15.8 / 21.0 / 28.1% (left), 5.9 / 10.9 / 15.6 / 17.0 / 20.9 / 24.7% (right). Leave-one-out check (predict a contact index by a line between its two neighbouring contact indices): error median 0.01 m, 90th percentile 0.06 to 0.08 m for spans up to 20 m, 0.12 to 0.16 m for 20 to 30 m, and 0.06 to 0.15 m up to 130 m, but with only 8 to 19 cases per span class beyond 30 m and LMU's width stepping every 4 to 10 m, so 30 m is kept as the length the data supports (a longer limit would gain about 4% of the grid per 10 m). Bridged metres are *not* counted as `edgeMeasured` (the debug tint and the meaning "a wheel crossed here" stay honest); `surveyHalfWidths` returns them as `leftBridged` / `rightBridged`.
- **Coverage with bridging (grid of 6,980 m):** left 21.9% contact, 7.0% bridged, 71.1% LMU fallback; right 23.0% contact, 11.2% bridged, 65.8% fallback. Bridging adds less than the 15 m crossing spacing suggests because the crossings are not spread evenly along the lap.
- **Largest step between neighbouring metres:** 1.13 m on the left at metre 271 (La Source; fallback to fallback: LMU has no sample for 17 m there and the smoothed width swings around its single value; LMU raw steps by 5.19 m) and 0.35 m on the right at metre 245 (fallback to contact; LMU raw 3.60 m).
- **Third survey, weave laps (four captures; 2026-11-06):** one lap per side, the outer tyres weaving between the tarmac and the run-off on the straights, the inner tyres staying on the tarmac, 455 to 512 s per lap. Crossings per lap rose from about 410 to 460 (outer wheels held over the edge) to 1,861 (left) and 1,961 (right). Result: 8,381 edge points; **left 43.3% contact, 49.6% bridged, 7.2% LMU fallback; right 44.1% contact, 42.5% bridged, 13.5% fallback.** |contact - LMU| median 0.16 / 0.17 m, 90th percentile 0.71 / 0.76 m. Longest stretches without a contact: 54 m (left, near Paul Frère), 85 m (right, on the Kemmel straight). Largest step between neighbouring metres: 0.92 m on the left at metre 254 (La Source, contact to fallback), 0.28 m on the right. Front/rear crossing agreement: median 0.33 m, 90th percentile 0.81 m (2,491 pairs). The figures above for the first and second surveys are kept for comparison.
- **Spa test:** `lib/trackSurvey.spa.test.ts` checks the three above, reporting the contact / bridged / fallback shares per side and the largest step with its metre, bounding the step between neighbouring metres at 1.25 m (observed 1.13 m, limit chosen between that and the 3.6 to 5.2 m of LMU's raw width).

LMU's width (first pass):

- **Coverage:** an 18-car session on the racing line measured both edges on 22% of the lap. Four survey laps (two keeping about 1 m inside each white line) raised it to 99.4% in 5 m stretches. On the 1 m grid, scoring (5 Hz, about 5.6 m between samples at 100 km/h) leaves single metres empty, so gaps of up to 8 m between samples are interpolated; then each edge is measured on 99.1% (left) and 99.4% (right) of the grid and both on the same metre on 98.6%. The remaining gaps are real (around La Source and the start line) and are filled by the usual gap filling in `trackEdges.ts`.
- **Left and right differ:** the half-widths from LMU's centre path differ by more than 1 m in two thirds of 5 m stretches, so one side cannot be mirrored onto the other.
- **Cars agree:** for one 5 m stretch and side, different cars report the same edge within 0.05 m (median).
- **Surface types:** type 6 is the flat kerb (`RMBLFLAT`); type 5 is the razor kerb (`RMBLRAZOR`) but also drains and concrete (`COBL,DRAIN`, `CRETE`). On one flying lap, wheels touched flat kerbs in 71 ten-metre stretches against 33 for type 5. The recording only has the type (at 5 Hz, 8 to 11 m between samples at 150 to 200 km/h), so `tools/extract-laps.mjs` now counts 5 or 6; the shared-memory terrain names tell kerbs from drains and give 100 Hz.
- **Flat kerbs sit on `mTrackEdge`** within about 0.5 m (wheels assumed 0.85 m either side of the car centre; wheel positions are not in shared memory, so a kerb hit is placed at the car's position and side).

## Limits

- One track, one session type: the survey file is per track and layout, and needs someone to drive it.
- Kerb runs still come from kerb hits at the car's position (accurate to about a metre along the lap); their width comes from the `kerb_outer` points where there are at least three on the run, else 0.9 m. `other` kerbs are drawn like the others.
- Contacts only exist where a wheel crossed the edge: with the second survey that is 22% and 23% of the lap, plus 7% and 11% bridged across gaps of at most 30 m; elsewhere the edge is LMU's width shifted to meet the measured ends.
- Tarmac to kerb is counted as the edge, so an apex kerb that sits on the racing surface is part of the edge and not of the road. The two wheels of a car are 1.7 m apart, so a wheel crossing says nothing about a stretch that no wheel crossed.
- Half-track (0.85 m) and the split of the axles are priors for the McLaren 720S GT3; another car needs `--half-track`, `--front-z` and `--rear-z`.
